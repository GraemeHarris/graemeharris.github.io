Infinite Jest caught me at the exact moment in a young man's life as it seems
to catch most young men - mid 20's and full of strife and pretentious
challenge. I want to read the hard book - everyone talks about it.

I feel like reviewing Infinite Jest is in part shouting in the howl of a
thousand better reviews, but also part meditative walk along the comfortably
worn wooden steps of old.

The book is pretentious. I like how pretentious it is. I love how sarcastic and
biting the parodies are. But most importantly the jest within, the addiction,
shines through. Infinite Jest is the best representation of addiction and the
minds of addicts I have read.

What is important about the addicts in Infinite Jest is not just their real
gritty suffering. A lot of books love to pour over those issues and give the
reader a voyeur's pleasure. Instead the main aspect of addiction that I found so
compelling in Infinite Jest is the build up. There is a section focussed on how
the PGOAT has found herself completely addicted to free basing cocaine.

When she is heading to a party she has no intention of using. But as she walks
toward the party, she finds her hands absently pulling together the components
she needs to build up to an improvised high. Her frontal thinking is in denial.
The addiction has become primal.

If you have ever been an addict, or lived within an addict's household, these
stories will hit you cleanly in the soul. I hope you find peace. Our brains seem
so hackable.
